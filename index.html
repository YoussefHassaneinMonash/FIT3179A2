15 Seasons, 18 Clubs: What Really Wins?

Since Greater Western Sydney joined in 2012, the AFL has had 18 clubs. Every one of them has played every season since. That makes the last 15 years a fair comparison. So who has dominated, does playing at home really help, and what do winning teams do differently?

Footy terms in 30 seconds

Ladder
The league table. Teams are ranked by premiership points (4 for a win, 2 for a draw), then by percentage.
Percentage
Points scored ÷ points conceded × 100. It's the tie-breaker when teams have equal points.
Finals
The end-of-season series for the top 8 (top 10 since 2026). The top four get a second chance if they lose their first final.
Premiership / flag
Winning the Grand Final, the last game of the season.
Inside 50
Moving the ball into your attacking zone, the 50-metre arc in front of goal.
Clearance
Winning the ball out of a stoppage (a bounce or throw-in) and sending it forward.
Contested possession
Winning the ball while under physical pressure from an opponent.
Margin
How many points the winning team won by.
Home-and-away season
The regular season, before finals. Every game has a home team and an away team.
Gather Round
Since 2023, all nine games of one round are played in and around Adelaide.
Hub season
2020, when COVID-19 moved about half of all games to Queensland hubs, many with no crowd.
Three-peat
Winning three premierships in a row.

How to read the colours

Chapter 01

Who ruled the era?

Three clubs won three flags each: Hawthorn, Richmond and the Brisbane Lions. But winning premierships and being consistently good are not the same thing.

Geelong: never far from the top

Geelong won only one flag (2022). Yet it has the best average ladder finish of any club (4.3), and it played finals in 13 of the 15 seasons.

Dynasties don't last. Richmond won three flags between 2017 and 2020, then finished 18th in 2024. Hawthorn, after its three-peat (2013–15), had slumped to 16th by 2023.

Gold Coast, which joined in 2011, has played finals only once (2025).

Pick a club from the menu to trace its journey up and down the ladder.

Consistency beats a hot streak

Geelong (225 wins) and Sydney (212) top the win tally by a clear margin. Both have been to plenty of Grand Finals but won just one each.

Over 15 years, steady seasons add up to more wins than a short golden era. Hawthorn, with three flags, is only third.

At the other end, Gold Coast won 110 games in 15 seasons, fewer than half of Geelong's total.

Chapter 02

The geography of footy

Ten of the 18 clubs are Victorian, but the game is played from Perth to the Gold Coast, and even in Darwin, Alice Springs, Shanghai and Wellington.

Footy-mad states

In 2026, games in Victoria drew about 67 fans for every 100 Victorians. The count includes visiting fans, so it measures how much footy a state hosts. South Australia was close behind at 64, helped by Gather Round, when a whole round is played in and around Adelaide.

In New South Wales and Queensland, the rugby league states, it was fewer than 10.

Chapter 03

Does home ground matter?

Every club wins more often at home, but the size of that advantage varies a lot.

The Kardinia Park fortress

Geelong won 82% of its home games. Its compact ground suits its own players and is unfamiliar to visitors. Away from home, it wins 56%, still among the best.

Gold Coast has the biggest gap of all: it wins 47% at home but only 21% away.

Collingwood has no home advantage at all: 58% at home, 58% away.

That's because many of Collingwood's “away” games are played close to home. More than half are in Melbourne, and 42% are at the MCG itself, in front of its own fans.

Does travel decide away games?

The two West Australian clubs travel about 60,000 km a season, more than three times as far as Collingwood (16,000 km).

If distance were the main reason teams lose away, the dots would slope down to the right. They barely do. Fremantle travels the furthest of any club but has a better away record than the league average.

Sydney wins 63% of its away games, the best record, while travelling a middling 26,000 km.

The quality of the team matters far more than the kilometres.

Chapter 04

What do winners do differently?

Behind every win are hundreds of small contests. These charts compare clubs' average game stats to see which ones go with success.

Scoring separates the best from the rest

Averaged over all 15 seasons, top-four clubs kicked 12% more goals than the league average and took 10% more marks inside 50. Bottom-four clubs kicked 17% fewer.

The gaps in the “hard work” stats, like clearances, contested possessions and tackles, are much smaller, usually under 5% either way.

In 2026 the top four made almost exactly the league-average number of tackles, but kicked 17% more goals.

Chapter 05

Rivalries and close calls

Some match-ups are reliably one-sided. Meanwhile, games overall are getting tighter.

The most one-sided match-ups

Geelong beat Essendon in 16 of their 18 games. Sydney beat North Melbourne 19 times in 22.

The top rows are mostly teal, because the strongest clubs beat almost everyone. The colour gets browner as you move down to the weaker clubs.

Closer contests

In 2012 the typical (median) game was won by 34 points. By 2026 it was 25.

Close games are more common too. In 2012, 21% of games were decided by two goals (12 points) or less. In 2026 it was 28%.

Each ridge shows how common each winning margin was in one season. A tall peak near the left means lots of close games. A long tail to the right means lots of thrashings. The red marks show the median margin moving left over time.

Chapter 06

2026: a three‑peat, and who's rising

Brisbane beat Fremantle by seven points to win a third straight flag, matching Hawthorn's 2013–15 run. Behind them, the ladder had a major shake-up.

A shake-up on the ladder

Sydney climbed eight places in a year and Melbourne climbed seven. Greater Western Sydney went the other way, falling seven places.

Record crowds

In 2020, crowds were banned or capped all season, and 29 games had no crowd at all. Counting every game, the average fell to 6,377. By 2026 it had climbed to 39,126, the highest of the 15 seasons.
